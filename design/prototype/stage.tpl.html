Dawnblade Stage

A real engine game, Kayo against Dorinthea, staged the way the new table would show it.

Tap a card in your hand to peek, tap again for what a second tap does. Tap the table to skip the staging. Space plays and pauses.

Read it with no card knowledge

  • Sword is how hard a card hits; shield is how much it blocks; the coloured pips are what it is worth as money.
  • The hatched ghost on a life bar is what lands if both players pass now. It is the engine's own answer, run on a copy. It matched all 18 strikes in this game.
  • The equation under the ring is the whole fight: attack vs WALL → damage.
  • Dots under each bar are cards in hand. Only cards with a shield can block, so count the shields as well.
  • The lit portrait and clock show whose decision it is. Life numbers sit beside the bars, so nothing ever covers the end of the bar where damage shows.

What is real, what is mocked

  • Real: every life total, card, wall, chain link and caption comes from judge.reduce on seed p1. The judge audited all 0 states and found 0 problems.
  • Mocked: think time. The clocks charge each player a simulated pause per decision so you can see the 15:00 bank and average time work.
  • The caption keeps the headline lines. The full feed stays in the log, and this prototype applies the planned fix: a face-down arsenal card is never named.

The three presets

  • Story (first ten games): full staging, the equation, plain-English ghost label, and the rules colour commentator.
  • Competitor (default on a clock): fast staging, headline captions, numbers only.
  • Instant: no staging at all. The board snaps and the clock never waits on an animation.
